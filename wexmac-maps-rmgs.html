Deltek estimates solicitation 07/2026 and award 09/2026. Marked High Priority in GovWin on 06/16/2026.

The requirement

A Market Research & Request for Information (FAR Part 10) to strengthen judicial security systems and institutional protection for Ecuador’s Judicial Council (Consejo de la Judicatura) and the Ecuadorian National Police. Four objectives: (1) technical security solutions — standardized operating procedures (SOPs), risk-management systems, institutional coordination mechanisms; (2) workforce development and training for operational readiness; (3) monitoring & evaluation methodologies for compliance, accountability, and continuous improvement; (4) alignment with Ecuadorian legal frameworks and international standards. Annex A adds a centralized risk-information system shared across institutions, information-exchange protocols, risk-classification and protection-planning tools for justice officials, and a full workforce package (needs assessment, curriculum design, personnel vetting, training and simulation exercises, certification and evaluation).

The play

Institutional capacity building and security-force assistance that hardens partner justice institutions — SOP development, risk-based protection planning, an interagency information-sharing architecture, and a certified training pipeline. Pairs directly with the adjacent Ecuador port-city SFA pursuit into a single institutional-strengthening story for DoS WHA/INL.

Capability fit — Capacity Development / SFA / Protective Security

Capacity-development, training & curriculum, and advising capability; risk assessment, threat management, and protection-planning lines from the protective-security portfolio; M&E and institutional-strengthening delivery.

Vehicle & path

Respond to the DoS INL MR&RFI to shape the requirement; position for the estimated July 2026 solicitation via DoS INL / WHA vehicles and GLOBALCAP peace-and-stability task orders, with GATA II antiterrorism training via subsidiary Linxx. Full & Open — no set-aside — so lead with a strong technical solution and a deliverable-linked cost approach.

Projected Performance Work Statement. Constructed by RMGS from the RFI’s four stated objectives, the Section 6 information requests, and Annex A. No official government PWS has been released (Pre-RFP) — task areas below are the analytically-derived scope RMGS expects the solicitation to contain.

1.0
Program Management & Coordination

Governance, integrated work-planning, staffing and key-personnel management, quality control/quality assurance, risk and schedule management, financial and FAR/DoS compliance, and serving as primary liaison to INL Ecuador / U.S. Embassy Quito and to Ecuadorian counterparts (Judicial Council, National Police, Fiscalía). Establishes the reporting cadence and the deliverable-acceptance process that governs every other task area.

2.0
Judicial Security SOPs & Standardization

Develop, socialize, and support adoption of standardized operating procedures and institutional security policies for the protection of judges, prosecutors, courthouses, and judicial proceedings. Baseline current practice, draft SOPs aligned to Ecuadorian law and international standards, and build the institutional-coordination mechanisms that make the SOPs enforceable across the Judicial Council and National Police. (RFI Objective 2.2.1.)

3.0
Risk-Management System & Threat Assessment

Design and stand up a structured risk-management methodology: threat identification and assessment for justice-sector personnel and infrastructure, risk-classification criteria, and a repeatable assessment process. Directly answers the RFI’s call to “identify, assess, and manage threats affecting justice sector personnel and infrastructure” and HRW’s recommendation for a comprehensive risk-assessment system.

4.0
Protection Planning for Justice Officials

Translate assessed risk into protection plans and protective-measure standards for at-risk officials — personal-protection concepts, facility and courtroom hardening standards, and protocols to adjust protection as threats evolve. Includes the protection-planning tools the Annex A explicitly requires.

5.0
Centralized Risk-Information System & Interagency Coordination

Establish a centralized system to collect and share risk information across institutions, with defined information-exchange protocols and interagency coordination mechanisms. This is a secure data/IT build (architecture, access controls, interoperability) plus the governance to operate it — the deliverable most staffing plans under-resource.

6.0
Workforce Development & Training

Full training pipeline: training-needs assessment, curriculum design, personnel-vetting protocols, delivery of classroom and simulation exercises, and a certification-and-evaluation system so judicial-security personnel demonstrably hold the required knowledge and skills. (RFI Objective 2.2.2 and Annex A workforce component.)

7.0
Monitoring & Evaluation

Design and operate an M&E framework aligned to expected results: indicators, data collection, periodic compliance evaluations, corrective-action processes, and continuous-improvement reviews. Trains counterpart personnel to apply the framework so it endures past the period of performance. (RFI Objective 2.2.3.)

8.0
Legal-Framework Alignment & Compliance

Ensure every SOP, tool, and information system conforms to Ecuadorian constitutional and criminal-procedure law, personal-data-protection rules, and applicable international standards. Cross-cuts all task areas and is a stated evaluation dimension (RFI Objective 2.2.4 and Section 6.6).

Judicial Security & Rule of Law Under Fire
Assassinations · narco-infiltration · protection gaps
Targeted killings
A Wave Against Justice Officials
Prosecutor César Suárez was shot dead in Guayaquil in Jan 2024 while investigating the TC Televisión studio takeover. Others reported killed include prosecutor Leonardo Palacios (Durán, 2024), judge Marcos Mendoza (Oct 2025), and Manta prosecutor Alexandra Bravo (2026) — the third Manta prosecutor killed since 2022. Many victims were traveling without protective details.
Narco-infiltration
Metástasis · Purga · Plaga
The Metástasis case (opened Dec 2023 under AG Diana Salazar) exposed a network of judges, prosecutors, police, and officials — including a former Judicial Council president (Wilman Terán) — selling verdicts to jailed traffickers. The Purga and Plaga cases produced further sentences; collectively 25+ judicial officials were dismissed.
Protection gap
No Armor, No System
HRW (June 2026) found at-risk judges/prosecutors often lack armored vehicles or basic protective equipment, some provinces cannot run ballistics or remove bodies from scenes, and there are 1,000+ vacancies. Its top recommendation — a comprehensive risk-assessment system and effective protection — maps almost verbatim onto RFI001J.
Institutional note: Crusading AG Diana Salazar left office at term’s end (May 2025); deputy Wilson Toainga took over provisionally amid a stalled selection process — a leadership vacuum at the top of the prosecuting institution just as the threat peaks.
04
Security Crisis & Armed Criminal Groups
Choneros · Lobos · CJNG/Sinaloa · prisons
⚑
Internal Armed Conflict (Jan 9, 2024)
After the escape of Los Choneros leader “Fito” (José Adolfo Macías), Noboa declared a state of internal armed conflict and designated 22 gangs as terrorist organizations, deploying the military. The same day, ~13 gunmen stormed the TC Televisión studio live on air (13 later charged with terrorism).
◎
Fragmented, Cartel-Linked Landscape
Los Lobos (aligned with Mexico’s CJNG and Albanian/European networks) has overtaken Los Choneros (Sinaloa-linked). Splinters — Tiguerones, Chone Killers, Tiburones — fight for port cities. Fito was recaptured in a Manta bunker (June 2025) and extradited to the U.S. (EDNY, July 2025).
↑
Record Violence
Homicides hit a record ~45.7/100k in 2023, dipped in 2024 (~38), then rebounded to a new record in 2025 (~9,200 murders, ~50.9/100k) — the most violent year in Ecuador’s history. Durán’s rate has been cited near ~140/100k. Early-2026 government figures claim a decline (treat as a government claim).
▦
Prisons as Gang Command Nodes
400+ deaths in prison violence since 2021 across mass-casualty riots; criminal orgs run facilities, extort inmates, and obtained firearms, grenades, and explosives. The military now controls prisons under recurring states of exception.

Cocaine Economy & Critical Infrastructure
Guayaquil · Posorja · maritime exit
Transit hub
~70% of Andean Cocaine
Colombian and Peruvian cocaine funnels to Ecuador’s coast and out through Guayaquil and the deep-water Posorja port to Europe and the U.S. European seizures traced to Posorja spiked in 2024–2025, making it a top loading port for Rotterdam/Antwerp seizures.
Methods
Semi-Subs & Port Infiltration
Speedboats, semi-submersibles, container contamination, and “Spidermen” who rope drugs onto departing ships. Port infiltration corrupts municipal bodies, customs, and security — an institutional-integrity problem, not only an interdiction one.
Critical infrastructure
Coca Codo Sinclair Risk
The Chinese-financed Coca Codo Sinclair dam supplies roughly one-third of national electricity but is plagued by thousands of cracks and river-erosion risk — a strategic single point of failure adjacent to the security picture.

U.S.–Ecuador Cooperation & SOUTHCOM Engagement
INL · SOFA · joint operations · foreign-bases vote
$
Funded & Growing Assistance
A $25M security-cooperation agreement (2024–2025) sits atop $81M+ since 2018 in counter-narcotics/TOC assistance. State’s Integrated Country Strategy explicitly backs judicial and prison reform. Los Choneros and Los Lobos were designated Foreign Terrorist Organizations (2025, as reported).
⚓
Agreements & Joint Operations
A 2023 Status-of-Forces and maritime “ship-rider” agreement (ratified Feb 2024) underpins expanding cooperation. 2025–2026 saw joint operations (Fito’s capture, coastal strikes) and — as reported — a U.S. military operation in Ecuador in early 2026. SOUTHCOM funded ~$13M in air-surveillance radar modernization.
✕
Foreign-Bases Referendum Rejected
In a Nov 2025 referendum, voters rejected lifting the constitutional ban on foreign military bases (~60% “no”), blocking a U.S. return to the former Manta base. Cooperation therefore runs through advisory, assistance, and capacity-building channels — the lane RFI001J occupies.
⚑
Noboa & Private Security
Noboa (re-elected April 2025, ~56%) pursues militarized “mano dura” and engaged Blackwater founder Erik Prince as a security advisor. With USAID dissolved (July 2025), civilian rule-of-law programming consolidated under State/INL — sharpening the case for contracted capacity.

2025–2026 Reforms & Foreign Influence
Solidarity & Intelligence laws · Plan Fénix · China
New legal framework
Solidarity & Intelligence Laws
The National Solidarity Law and a new Intelligence Law (both June 2025) expand executive/armed-forces powers and create a new intelligence framework. Rights groups warn of weakened oversight — a compliance and institutional-design context any judicial-security program must navigate.
Flagship strategy
Plan Fénix (~$830M / 10 yr)
Noboa’s security plan centralizes intelligence and expands military presence at borders, ports, and airports with drones, radars, and satellites. Criticized as vague and undisclosed; 2025’s record violence undercut early results — leaving institutional strengthening as the durable complement.
PRC footprint
Trade, Mining & Chancay
The China–Ecuador FTA entered force May 2024; Chinese SOEs dominate oil and mining. Regionally, Peru’s Chinese-operated Chancay megaport (opened Nov 2024) reshapes Pacific logistics — the commercial-influence backdrop to U.S. re-engagement.
