01 The opportunity

Assistance as statecraft.

The Bureau of Near Eastern Affairs' Office of Assistance Coordination is competing up to $190M in Economic Support Funds and National Security Investment Program funds across nine MENA country programs. Priority sectors include energy, critical infrastructure, emerging technology, critical minerals, trade facilitation, workforce development, and economic recovery in conflict-affected areas. For-profit firms are eligible. Selection is a two-step process: a 1–3 page Statement of Interest, followed by an invited full proposal.

02 Why RMGS

The case for RMGS as prime.

Most priorities in this APS depend on security work: port access, trusted infrastructure, functioning customs, and institutions protected from malign influence. RMGS has performed that work for two decades as a federal prime, including for the Department of State.

01 · The customer

A standing DoS record

RMGS is a prime on the DoS Bureau of African Affairs Advisory & Reporting IDIQ ($500M), embedding subject-matter advisors inside host-government institutions and security forces with logistics, life support, and reporting. Through the RMGS family, it is also a prime on GATA II ($990M), Diplomatic Security's anti-terrorism assistance vehicle, delivering 80+ courses across 34 countries. The APS calls for the same delivery model in a neighboring region.

02 · The environments

Compliance where it's hardest

Iraq requires "capacity to operate in a complex security environment." Lebanon and Libya require strict vetting and monitoring so that no assistance reaches Hizballah, IAMGs, or other armed groups. RMGS holds independently audited certifications for private security operations (ISO 18788, ASIS PSC-1) along with ISO 9001 and CMMC Level 2, and performance in more than 60 countries, including the CENTCOM theater.

03 · The record

The scoring math

The review criteria weight organizational capacity at 35 of 100 points. RMGS brings 350+ executed contracts and task orders, $165M in federal prime performance, CPARS-documented delivery, and a portfolio that is 84% firm-fixed-price. Under EO 14332, tied scores break toward the applicant with the lower indirect rate.

04 Nine programs

Country by country.

The APS defines priorities for eight countries, the West Bank, and the region as a whole. Hover each program for its stated priority and where RMGS fits. Award ceilings per the APS; floors are $250K throughout.

NEA / 01

Algeria

Port security · natural resources · AI · commercial investment
Ceiling $20M
The priority

Develop hydrocarbon and critical-minerals potential with U.S. partners, connect AI infrastructure to trusted vendors, and export Algerian security expertise to stabilize the Sahel.

The RMGS fit

Port and harbor security assessments for the export gateways, and the embedded-advisor model RMGS runs for DoS in Africa today, which maps to Algeria's goal of exporting security expertise to the Sahel.

NEA / 02

Bahrain

Energy security · network security · supply chain · fintech
Ceiling $20M
The priority

Deepen the U.S. economic relationship and advance Abraham Accords regional integration across defense systems, energy, digital infrastructure, and financial services.

The RMGS fit

Threat and vulnerability assessment for energy and network infrastructure, exercise programs that validate resilience, and a long working history with the U.S. Navy.

NEA / 03

Iraq

Border security · customs modernization · CI protection · IAMG-free implementation
Ceiling $20M
The priority

Keep IAMGs out of government spending, modernize borders and customs, protect critical infrastructure, and build transparent markets. Implementers must be able to operate in Iraq's complex security environment.

The RMGS fit

The Iraq eligibility requirements match RMGS's operating model: ISO 18788 / PSC-1 audited vetting and monitoring, counterterrorism-compliance experience from GATA II, border and interdiction training, and critical-infrastructure protection from assessment through exercise.

NEA / 04

Lebanon

Energy & telecom infrastructure · Hizballah service substitution · strict vetting
Ceiling $30M
The priority

Support sovereignty by substituting Hizballah-provided services and securing energy and telecom infrastructure, under strict vetting so no assistance benefits armed groups.

The RMGS fit

Lebanon carries the APS's largest ceiling and its heaviest compliance requirements. RMGS's independently audited security-operations management (ISO 18788, PSC-1) is designed for this monitoring standard.

NEA / 05

Libya

Institutional unification · energy governance · civilian-only vetting
Ceiling $20M
The priority

Promote unification of political, economic, and security institutions and foster energy and defense-sector growth, with strict vetting ensuring assistance reaches civilian institutions only.

The RMGS fit

Security-sector advisory and institutional capacity building, supported by independently audited vetting and monitoring practices that meet the civilian-only assistance requirement.

NEA / 06

Morocco

World Cup 2030 buildout · tech · energy · Sahel stabilization
Ceiling $20M
The priority

Capture the World Cup 2030 infrastructure buildout for U.S. firms across energy, cybersecurity, aviation, rail, and ports, while contributing to Sahel stability.

The RMGS fit

RMGS currently supports FIFA 2026 preparations, with 30+ host-city readiness exercises and a league-mandated venue security assessment. That work is directly relevant to Morocco's World Cup 2030 buildout.

NEA / 07

Oman

Technology · space · critical minerals · sanctions enforcement
Ceiling $20M
The priority

Grow emerging sectors with U.S. firms, leverage Oman's geography outside the Strait of Hormuz, and support enforcement of sanctions against Iran.

The RMGS fit

Maritime interdiction and VBSS instruction supporting sanctions enforcement, plus port-security depth for the trade infrastructure Oman's location makes strategic.

NEA / 08

Tunisia

Grid modernization · ICT · Bizerte port & logistics · procurement reform
Ceiling $20M
The priority

Position U.S. firms and standards in infrastructure, logistics, and technology. Bizerte is named as a strategic port and logistics node, and grid modernization is a priority sector.

The RMGS fit

Port and logistics-node security assessment, plus energy-infrastructure exercise experience relevant to Tunisia's grid modernization priority.

NEA / 09

West Bank

Workforce readiness · ICT links · reducing malign-actor conditions
Ceiling $20M
The priority

Build private-sector capacity and workforce readiness aligned with U.S. business needs to reduce conditions exploited by malign actors.

The RMGS fit

Workforce training at scale: curriculum, instructor cadres, and measurable outcomes, delivered with strict vetting. Note that local registration is required, so this program is strongest as a supporting line of effort.

Regional

Cross-cutting

Strengthen regional energy integration, support trade corridors like IMEC, and promote cross-border cooperation that expands market access and reduces isolation.

Trade corridors run through ports, border crossings, and infrastructure nodes. RMGS's assessment, exercise, and training work applies at each of them and can be organized as a corridor-level security program.

SOI / COVER

Critical details

Title · organization · country · dollars · duration
Sets the frame
In the SOI

"Securing the Buildout: U.S.-Standard Event-Security Readiness for World Cup 2030." RMGS, Inc. (SDVOSB). Target country: Morocco. Federal share: $8.5M over 30 months, with a modest corporate cost share.

Why

The Morocco ceiling is $20M. Entering well below it signals cost discipline, and the cover table sits outside the page limit, so every scope signal here costs no narrative space.

SOI / A

Issue / opportunity

The problem statement, framed commercially
~½ page
In the SOI

Morocco's World Cup 2030 buildout will award security-heavy tenders across venues, transport, and energy. European and Chinese vendors are positioned; opaque procurement disadvantages U.S. firms. The readiness gap is the opening.

Why

Quality of program idea carries 50 of 100 points, and it is scored on responsiveness to the framework. This frames a commercial-diplomacy problem, which is what the APS funds, rather than a security-assistance problem.

SOI / B

Solution & activities

Three activity lines, all currently performed on contract
~1 page
In the SOI

Three lines: (1) HSEEP-derived host-city and venue readiness exercises; (2) venue and infrastructure security assessments to U.S. standards (ISO 31000, design-basis threat); (3) security workforce training with U.S.-aligned technical specifications that feed tender documents.

Why

Every activity is one RMGS already performs on contract, for FIFA 2026, DHS/CISA, and GATA II customers. Feasibility can be shown, not asserted.

SOI / B.1

Outcomes & results

Defining "success" in the APS's own terms
~½ page
In the SOI

Countable results: exercises delivered, Moroccan personnel certified, assessments adopted, tenders carrying U.S.-aligned specifications, and U.S. vendor engagements. Success is defined as Moroccan authorities procuring to U.S. standards.

Why

The APS requires outcomes that advance the 2025 NSS, the ASP 2026–2030, and the NEA Regional Strategic Brief. Export wins and reduced adversary technology penetration are that language, stated measurably.

SOI / B.2

Theory of change

The if / then / because sentence
~¼ page
In the SOI

If Morocco's World Cup 2030 security readiness is built and validated on U.S. standards, then its ministries and venue authorities will specify and procure U.S. technology and services, because procurement follows the standards and training already embedded. Evidence: the FIFA 2026 host-city engagement and standards-adoption patterns in GATA II partner programs.

Why

Section D asks for this statement in this form, with supporting evidence. Providing it verbatim removes a reviewer checkbox and anchors the logic framework in Annex 1.

SOI / C

Partners

None — direct implementation
~⅛ page
In the SOI

No subrecipients. RMGS implements directly, with Moroccan government counterparts engaged as beneficiaries rather than pass-through entities.

Why

A single accountable implementer means cleaner vetting, no pass-through cost, and faster mobilization. It also concentrates the organizational-capacity score on RMGS's own record instead of diluting it.

SOI / D

Cost share

A modest, deliverable pledge
~⅛ page
In the SOI

Preliminary in-kind contribution: exercise-design intellectual property, corporate SME bench time, and reachback infrastructure, per 2 CFR 200.306.

Why

Cost share is not required but is favorably considered in merit review. A modest pledge RMGS can document earns that consideration without creating audit risk.

SOI / ANNEX

Format & annexes

Where the evidence goes
4 × 1 page
In the SOI

Three-page narrative; four annexes: (1) a program logic model tracing the project along the APS framework shown under The Opportunity, (2) past-performance matrix with contract numbers, (3) 30-month timeline, (4) M&E indicator table.

Why

Reviewers stop reading at the page limit, and charts and annexes are excluded from it. The evidence load goes to annexes; the narrative stays free for the argument.

06 Path to award

From SOI to start of work.

Phase one is a 1–3 page Statement of Interest via MyGrants, reviewed on a rolling basis. Selected applicants are invited to submit a full proposal, with possible co-design. The criteria weight organizational capacity and feasibility, both documented above.
