01 The CISA ISD requirement

Full-spectrum infrastructure exercise support, to HSEEP standard.

CISA's Infrastructure Security Division (ISD) requires full-spectrum infrastructure exercise support delivered in accordance with the Homeland Security Exercise and Evaluation Program (HSEEP). HSEEP defines two families of exercises — and RMGS has planned, conducted, and evaluated both across the homeland-security enterprise for more than a decade.

Discussion-based

Familiarize

Seminars, workshops, facilitated discussions, tabletop exercises (TTX), and games — familiarizing players with the plans, policies, agreements, and procedures that protect infrastructure.

Operations-based

Validate

Drills, functional exercises, and full-scale exercises — validating in action the plans, policies, agreements, and procedures reviewed in the discussion-based events.

Across both families, RMGS delivers the full skill spectrum ISD calls for: programmatic and administrative support — program management, front-office support, strategic planning and communications, stakeholder coordination, and technical document development — together with research and analysis support, exercise-related training, and physical and communications exercise planning and conduct. That record runs from $3.2M of HSEEP-compliant exercise work for the DHS infrastructure-protection mission, through CISA ISD and FEMA National Exercise support, to 30-plus tabletop and functional exercises for the 2026 FIFA World Cup and NFL host-city mission.

03 The sixteen sectors

We work where CISA works.

CISA designates sixteen critical infrastructure sectors whose assets, systems, and networks are considered so vital that their incapacitation would have a debilitating effect on national security, economic security, or public health and safety. RMGS has delivered exercises, assessments, adversary emulation, or protective security across nine of them — sector numbering per the CISA framework.

SEC / 02

Commercial Facilities

Delivered — venue security, FIFA & NFL
What RMGS has done

Over 30 tabletop and functional exercises preparing FIFA World Cup 2026 host-city venues and stakeholders for game-day conditions, plus a league-mandated pre-season security certification assessment of a professional venue, mapped to ISO 31000.

Why it matters

Stadiums and mass gatherings are the Nation's most visible soft targets — a game-day failure is measured in lives, not headlines.

SEC / 03

Communications

Delivered — lifeline exercises
What RMGS has done

Host-city lifeline exercises that stress communications under live injects — degrading networks alongside power, water, and public safety to expose where coordination actually breaks.

Why it matters

Every other sector's response runs on communications. When comms degrade, command and control fails first — and everything downstream follows.

SEC / 06

Defense Industrial Base

Delivered — DoD & DTRA programs
What RMGS has done

Two decades inside DoD programs — DTRA nuclear-enterprise support, CWMD test & evaluation, and adversary emulation at defense facilities, with a Top Secret facility clearance and a majority-cleared workforce.

Why it matters

The DIB builds and sustains the deterrent. Adversaries target it continuously, below the threshold of war — through its facilities, people, and supply chains.

SEC / 07

Emergency Services

Delivered — FEMA, VDEM, first responders
What RMGS has done

HSEEP-compliant exercise design, facilitation, and after-action reporting for DHS and the Commonwealth of Virginia (VDEM, VDOT), FEMA capability validation, and CBRN first-responder training.

Why it matters

Emergency services is the sector that catches every other sector's failure — its readiness is the difference between an incident and a catastrophe.

SEC / 08

Energy

Delivered — grid & energy-node exercises
What RMGS has done

Exercises stressing power and energy nodes under live injects for host cities and infrastructure stakeholders, and counter-sUAS threat emulation around the fixed sites — substations and energy nodes — that warfighting power depends on.

Why it matters

The grid is the dependency under everything else: no power means no water, no communications, no defense. It fails as a system, not a site.

SEC / 11

Government Facilities

Delivered — red team at protected sites
What RMGS has done

Red-team adversary emulation and design-basis-threat assessment at some of the Nation's most tightly controlled federal sites — testing physical security the way a capable adversary actually would.

Why it matters

These facilities concentrate the people, information, and continuity-of-government functions adversaries prize most. Checklist security doesn't find what an adversary finds.

SEC / 14

Nuclear Reactors, Materials & Waste

Delivered — adversary emulation, SMR security
What RMGS has done

Adversary role-player support at one of the most tightly controlled nuclear facilities in the world, infiltration of a national nuclear research facility, SMR security red-teaming, and an active $8.0M DTRA nuclear-enterprise task order.

Why it matters

No sector carries higher consequence — and the coming wave of SMR deployments multiplies the sites, and the attack surface, that must be defended from day one.

SEC / 15

Transportation Systems

Delivered — host-city & VDOT programs
What RMGS has done

Host-city transportation readiness exercises for FIFA 2026, VDOT VIPREP support, and $6.5M+ training the Coast Guard teams that protect ports and waterways — the maritime backbone of the sector.

Why it matters

Nothing moves without it: people, freight, and military logistics ride the same networks, and a disruption anywhere cascades everywhere.

SEC / 16

Water & Wastewater

Delivered — lifeline exercises
What RMGS has done

Lifeline exercises stressing water and wastewater response under live injects for host cities and utility stakeholders — alongside power, communications, and public safety.

Why it matters

Water is the least-defended lifeline with the least redundancy. A city can improvise around most failures — days without water isn't one of them.

04 Special events & mass gatherings

Ready for the big event.

Stadiums, arenas, and convention centers are Commercial Facilities Sector infrastructure — and on event day the gathering itself becomes the thing that must be protected. RMGS prepares venues and their public-safety partners for large-scale gatherings of every kind: professional and collegiate sporting events, concerts and festivals, conventions and conferences — aligned to CISA's special-event and mass-gathering guidance, including the Special Event Assessment Rating (SEAR) process.

Assess

Venue & event risk assessment

Design-basis-threat and vulnerability assessments of the venue, perimeter, and event footprint — feeding the SEAR picture and the security plan.

Exercise

Game-day tabletops & drills

Tabletop, functional, and full-scale exercises against the scenarios that matter: active threat, evacuation and shelter-in-place, severe weather, medical surge, and cyber-physical disruption.

Coordinate

Unified command & the seam

Rehearsing coordination across venue operations, law enforcement, fire and EMS, emergency management, and the leagues — so no single agency owns the seam alone on event day.

Communicate

Communications & continuity

Public-safety messaging, spectator notification, and communications resilience under peak crowd load — plus continuity if an event must pause, delay, or evacuate.

Emerging threat

Airspace & counter-UAS

A small drone over a full stadium is now a planning assumption. RMGS stress-tests detection, decision-making, and response for the airspace above the crowd.

Rehearse

Event-day readiness

Walk-throughs, rehearsals, and controller-run injects that put the plan under live conditions before the gates open.

Proven at national scale: RMGS is delivering 30-plus tabletop and functional exercises for the 2026 FIFA World Cup and the NFL host-city mission — stressing power, communications, water, transportation, and public-safety response for the venues and cities that host the world.
